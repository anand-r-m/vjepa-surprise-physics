## 4. Detection recompute (FACT)
Rule: per video full-clip max over windows of S.min over C; tau = 2e-4 x median over 360 clean full-clip-max = 1.0875e-4; d = S_edit - S_clean; 1 if d > tau, 0.5 if |d| <= tau, else 0. Label: status.json confirms label 0 = possible (e.g. O1/01/2, /4 are is_possible=true), 180 possible.
Possible videos (n = 180), tie-half detection: **vflip 0.2111** (fraction strictly up 0.206), hflip 0.3861, reversal 0.6083. Mean S clean 0.5413, vflip 0.5377, hflip 0.5404, reversal 0.5427. This reproduces the reported 21.1% exactly. Impossible videos (n=180): vflip 0.275, hflip 0.356, reversal 0.556. All 360: vflip 0.243. Alternative aggregation (mean over windows): vflip 0.272 (possible). G2 fails under every variant tried; it is not an aggregation choice.
Effect structure: vflip lowers S in every one of the 17 windows by 0.003-0.0055 on average (about 0.9%); hflip lowers by about 0.001; reversal is mixed in sign by window (+0.004 to -0.006). Between-video SD of full-clip max is 0.019, corr(clean, vflip full-max) = 0.979, so vflip changes surprise by under 1/4 of one between-video SD and almost deterministically rescales it. Clean impossible-minus-possible gap is only 0.0056, similar in size to the vflip shift.

## 5. Splice structure (facts vs inference)
FACTS: (a) For O1/01 I hashed every frame: impossible video 1 = frames 0-45 of possible 2 then 46-99 of possible 4; impossible 3 = possible 4 then possible 2 (every impossible frame byte-identical to a possible frame at the same t). This matches prereg section 12 (120 single splice, 60 double). (b) The per-frame operation vflip is a bijection on pixels, so flip(impossible_t) = flip(possible_t') at the same indices: the vflipped impossible video is exactly the splice of the vflipped possible videos, with the same switch time. Time reversal likewise preserves splice structure (switch time becomes 99 - t_switch, order of sources swapped). (c) The possible videos are not edited-and-then-compared against anything with an extra discontinuity: a vflipped possible video is still one continuous render.
INFERENCES: Vflip is applied to every frame, so it does not add a temporal discontinuity; any surprise on the clean impossible videos comes from the splice/violation, and that survives a pixel-wise flip unchanged. A vflipped possible clip is still temporally smooth, only with "objects falling upward" (and the ground plane/sky swapped). Whether a latent predictor trained on natural video would find upward-falling motion more unpredictable within 17-window / 16-frame clips is an empirical question; the full-clip max is dominated by window 0, 6-7, 12 (argmax histogram of clean S), i.e. by early-clip and occluder/entry dynamics that are largely scene-dependent and gravity-neutral over short spans (INFERENCE, not tested). Since flip leaves the first-frame appearance similarly in-distribution (and the encoder is flip-invariant to some degree: hflip also lowers S), a near-zero or slightly negative shift is not itself evidence of a bug. So the vflip-as-positive-control premise (that vflip produces a violation the model flags) is an assumption of the design, not something the splice structure guarantees; the data say M1's full-clip-max surprise does not respond to it in the expected direction. The splice structure implies nothing about whether vflip should RAISE surprise; it only shows vflip does not alter the possible/impossible structure.

## Verdict
Pipeline: OK within checked sample (independent implementation equals stored to <= 8e-4, below fp16/fp32 noise). Detection 21.1% independently reproduced. G2 failure is a property of the model/data/premise (M1 full-clip-max surprise drops slightly under vflip), not an edit-path bug. Not checked: M0r runs, other 354 videos, fp16 GPU path itself (covered indirectly by the clean fp16-vs-fp32 floor), 13 of 17 windows for 6 of 7 videos.
